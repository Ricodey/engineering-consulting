From concept to a product you can manufacture.

Mechanical design, electronics, and firmware, engineered as one system from the architecture through a release a factory can build.

Engineered as a system, from the architecture out.

The work starts with architecture. What the product must do, which off-the-shelf parts stay, and how power, sensing, the enclosure, and the firmware share one design.

Then the engineering itself. Schematics and board layout. Mechanical design, seals, and tolerances. Firmware written against that hardware, with bring-up and a production test. Released as fabrication data, source, and a bill of materials a factory can buy.

Selected work

From an idea to hardware you can build again.

01 — Field Node

A sealed instrument, from a concept and a development board.

A sensing idea and a microcontroller module. The module stayed. Around it we engineered the carrier, the power path, the seal stack, and firmware that sleeps until the measurement matters. The enclosure is what a factory builds from that design.

Architecture
Interfaces, the power budget, and the environment it has to survive.
Electronics
Schematic and layout of the carrier around the module.
Mechanics
A gasketed aluminium housing and the connector that passes the seal.
Firmware
Scheduled sensing, bring-up, and an image production can flash.

02 — Drive

An evaluation kit, redesigned for volume.

An evaluation board that ran on a lab supply. Re-engineered as a drive you can call up on a drawing: protection on the schematic, a power stage with a real heat path, and firmware with limits a machine builder can set.

Electronics
Protection, a power stage, and connectors on a board meant to ship.
Mechanics
A heat path and a mounting scheme calculated with the enclosure.
Firmware
Current limits, a state machine, and behaviour written for that hardware.
Release
A module a factory can assemble, test, and repeat.
