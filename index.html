01 — Field Node

A sealed instrument, from a concept and a development board.

A sensing idea and a microcontroller module. The module stayed. Around it we engineered the carrier, the power path, the seal stack, and firmware that sleeps until the measurement matters. The enclosure is what a factory builds from that design.

Architecture
Interfaces, the power budget, and the environment it has to survive.
Electronics
Schematic and layout of the carrier around the module.
Mechanics
A gasketed aluminium housing and the connector that passes the seal.
Firmware
Scheduled sensing, bring-up, and an image production can flash.

Why design for mass manufacture.

The reliability and the cost are settled in the design. A product drawn for volume is cheaper to build, and easier to build again.

  1. 01

    A smaller, professional form factor.

    The enclosure and the board are designed together, so the product is compact and finished.

  2. 02

    Fewer failures.

    A part made the same way every time fails less on the line, and less in the field.

  3. 03

    Less labour in each part.

    Assembly and the sequence of the build are designed, so the work in every unit comes down.

  4. 04

    Volume that gets cheaper.

    Fewer unique parts and a repeatable process are what make the cost fall as the quantity rises.

  5. 05

    A base for the next product.

    The circuit, the enclosure, and the firmware stay in the company, ready for the product that follows.
